# Semantic Propagation Audit Report

## 1. Executive Summary
This audit analyzes the trajectory of a discrete social event (the "Gossip Burst") as it propagates through the simulated collective unconscious and exerts influence on the agent's decision manifold. 

The results indicate a high initial impact followed by a rapid, non-linear decay in semantic relevance. While the vector itself undergoes significant "semantic drift" due to environmental noise, the effective influence on decision-making processes stabilizes at a low baseline after approximately 12-14 hours (Step 4).

## 4. Findings & Observations

### 4.1 Decay Rate Analysis
The "Decay Rate" exhibits a hyperbolic pattern. The most significant loss of influence occurs between **Step 0 and Step 2**, where the influence magnitude drops by ~75%. This suggests that the agent's decision manifold is highly sensitive to immediate, high-entropy social signals but possesses robust mechanisms to filter out sustained "noise" as time progresses.

### 4.2 Semantic Drift (Ω)
The semantic drift remains relatively constant (averaging ~0.098), indicating that the "meaning" of the gossip does not fundamentally change, but rather its *structural alignment* with the decision manifold is diluted by the accumulation of environmental noise and the natural normalization processes of the memory system.

### 4.3 Decision Manifold Impact
The "Influence Magnitude" effectively reaches a floor by **Step 4**. Observations suggest that once an event's magnitude drops below a critical threshold (~0.11), it no longer shifts the agent's decision vectors significantly, instead becoming part of the ambient "background noise" of the collective unconscious.

## 5. Conclusion
The social propagation of "Gossip Bursts" is characterized by high volatility and extreme temporal locality. For effective influence or control, social signals must be timed to coincide with critical decision-making windows, as their ability to alter the manifold decays exponentially within the first few hours of event onset.
