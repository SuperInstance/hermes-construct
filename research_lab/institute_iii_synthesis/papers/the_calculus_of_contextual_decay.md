## Abstract

In the architecture of high-dimensional information processing, "context" is traditionally viewed as a stable substrate of structured relationships. However, as information traverses temporal or architectural boundaries, it undergoes a non-linear transformation. We define this process as **Contextual Decay**. This paper formalizes the mathematical transition from *Structured Context*—characterized by explicit relational graph topology—to *Vectorized Resonance*—a state where information is no longer defined by discrete predicates but by its harmonic interaction with a latent manifold. We propose a new calculus to model this decay, treating it not as a loss of entropy, but as a phase transition from topography to frequency.

## 1. Introduction: The Fallacy of Information Persistence

The prevailing paradigm in information theory treats decay as noise: the stochastic degradation of a signal that obscures its structured meaning. In the context of large-scale neural architectures and multi-agent synthesis, this view is insufficient. Context does not merely "vanish"; it undergoes a structural collapse. 

When a structured context (a set of explicit rules, entities, and logical relations) is compressed or abstracted, the discrete edges of its relational graph dissolve. What remains is not "empty" space, but a density field. This paper argues that the utility of information in high-dimensional spaces is better measured by its *resonance* with the existing manifold than by its adherence to its original structural constraints.

## 2. Formalizing Structured Context

Let a **Structured Context** $\mathcal{C}_S$ be defined as a directed, weighted graph $G = (V, E, W)$, where:
- $V$ is a set of discrete semantic tokens (nodes).
- $E \subseteq V \times V$ is a set of relational edges.
- $W: E \to \mathbb{R}$ is a weight function representing the strength of the logical or semantic dependency.

The complexity of $\mathcal{C}_S$ is governed by its topology $\mathcal{T}$, which dictates the paths through which information can flow. In this state, meaning is *local* and *explicit*: to understand node $v_i$, one must traverse the edges $e_{ij}$ defined in $W$.

## 3. The Decay Operator: $\mathcal{D}_\tau$

We introduce the **Decay Operator** $\mathcal{D}_\tau$, which acts upon $\mathcal{C}_S$ over a temporal or abstraction parameter $\tau$. 

The decay process is not a simple attenuation of weights $W$. Instead, it is a diffusion process on the manifold. We model the evolution of the context density $\rho$ using a modified Fokker-Planck equation:

$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial \tau} = -\nabla \cdot (\mathbf{A}(\rho, \tau) \rho) + \nabla^2 (\mathbf{B}(\rho, \tau) \rho)$$

Where:
- $\mathbf{A}$ represents the *drift* toward the most probable semantic centers (the "gravity" of existing knowledge).
- $\mathbf{B}$ represents the *diffusion* (the loss of specific relational edges).

As $\tau \to \infty$, the discrete edges $E \to \emptyset$, and the graph $G$ loses its definition as a set of nodes and edges.

## 4. Transition to Vectorized Resonance

The terminal state of decay is not silence, but **Vectorized Resonance** $\mathcal{R}_V$. 

In $\mathcal{R}_V$, the context is no longer a graph, but a continuous probability distribution over a latent manifold $\mathcal{M}$. An input signal $s$ is no longer "parsed" against $\mathcal{C}_S$; it "vibrates" within $\mathcal{M}$.

We define **Resonance** $\mathcal{R}$ as the inner product of the signal's frequency spectrum and the manifold's local curvature:

$$\mathcal{R}(s, \mathcal{M}) = \int_{\gamma} \langle \mathcal{F}(s), \nabla \mathcal{M} \rangle \, d\gamma$$

Where $\mathcal{F}(s)$ is the Fourier transform of the semantic signal. In this regime, "understanding" is the degree to which a signal aligns with the fundamental modes of the context manifold.

## 5. Conclusion: Towards a Harmonic Epistemology

The Calculus of Contextual Decay suggests that the goal of advanced integration is not to preserve structure—which is inherently fragile—but to cultivate resonance. By understanding the mathematical path from structure to resonance, we can design systems that do not fight decay, but leverage it to transform rigid information into fluid, resonant intelligence.
